1. What FaceTrack does

Attendance that is taken by the camera, not by a register passed around the hall.

In most lecture halls attendance is still taken on paper. It eats lecture time, the sheets get lost, and students sign for friends who are not in the room. FaceTrack replaces that with a camera at the front of the class: a student looks at it, is recognised from their own face, and is marked present in under a second.

How the parts fit together

INPUT
Camera
The lecturer's webcam captures the room
→
PROCESSING
Browser
Finds each face and turns it into 128 numbers
→
MATCHING
Server
Compares against enrolled students on that course
→
OUTPUT
Register
Attendance saved, reports and CSV produced
Your camera footage never leaves your computer The browser converts each face into a list of 128 numbers — a "descriptor" — and only those numbers are sent to the server. No photograph of the classroom is ever uploaded, and a descriptor cannot be turned back into a picture of a face.

What it gives you

CapabilityWhat it means in practice
Instant attendanceNo roll call. The camera scans continuously while you teach.
No proxy attendanceA student must present their own face. Naming somebody is not enough — matching happens on the server, against templates the browser never sees.
No double entriesA student who stays in front of the camera for ten minutes still produces exactly one record. The database itself enforces this.
Late trackingAnyone recognised after your cut-off (15 minutes by default) is marked late rather than present.
Locked registersClosing a session freezes it. Attendance cannot be added to a lecture that has finished.
Reports & eligibilityAttendance percentage per student, flagged against the course requirement (75% by default), exportable as CSV.

2. Before you start

Three things to check before your first lecture.

2.1  What you need

RequirementDetail
BrowserGoogle Chrome, Microsoft Edge or Safari, kept up to date. Chrome gives the smoothest camera performance.
CameraAny built-in laptop webcam or USB camera. A camera you can angle towards the students works best.
InternetNeeded to reach the site and save attendance. Face detection itself runs on your own machine.
LightingFaces must be lit from the front. Students backlit by a window are the single most common cause of a missed face.

2.2  The first load takes a moment

The first time you open the Register student or live capture screen in a given browser, roughly 12 MB of face-recognition models download in the background. The camera button stays greyed out and reads Loading face detector… until this finishes — usually 10 to 40 seconds. The browser caches them afterwards, so it only happens once.

Open the capture screen a minute before your lecture starts That lets the models finish downloading while students are still settling, instead of during the first minute of class.

2.3  The camera only works on a secure address

Browsers refuse camera access unless the page is on https:// or localhost. The live site is on HTTPS, so it works:

AddressCamera
https://attendace-system-david.vercel.app/✓ Works
http://localhost:3000 (running it yourself)✓ Works
http://192.168.x.x:3000 (a LAN address)✗ Blocked by the browser

4. The screens, one by one

Every screen in the platform, what it is for, and exactly what to click.

4.1  Sign in

Everyone /login

The entry point. There is no student login and no public sign-up — accounts are created by an administrator.

  1. Open https://attendace-system-david.vercel.app/. You are redirected here automatically.
  2. Enter your email address and password from section 3.1.
  3. Press Sign in. You land on the Dashboard.

4.2  Dashboard

Everyone /dashboard

Your landing page and the fastest route back into a lecture that is already running.

  • Registered students — total on the system, with a warning chip counting those who still have no face template.
  • Courses — how many courses you are responsible for.
  • Open sessions — lectures currently accepting attendance.
  • Marked today — records saved today, split between face and manual.
  • Open lecture sessions — each has an Open camera button that takes you straight to capture.
  • Recent attendance — the last eight students marked anywhere in the system.

4.3  Students

Everyone /students

The full student roll, with each student's biometric enrolment status.

  1. Use the search box to filter by name, matriculation number or department as you type.
  2. Read the Face template column: a green “3 samples” badge means the student can be recognised; a red “Not enrolled” badge means they cannot.
  3. Click any student's name to open their profile.
  4. Click + Register student (top right) to add somebody new.

4.4  Register a student & enrol their face

Admin only /students/new

This is the most important screen in the system. A student who has never been enrolled here can never be recognised in class.

  1. Fill in the matriculation number and full name (both required). Email, department and level are optional but make reports easier to read.
  2. On the right, wait for the button to change from Loading face detector… to Turn on camera, then press it. Allow camera access when the browser asks.
  3. Sit the student squarely in frame, well lit, about an arm's length from the camera.
  4. Press Capture sample three times, following the prompt under the video each time:
    • Sample 1 — look straight at the camera
    • Sample 2 — turn your head slightly to the left
    • Sample 3 — turn your head slightly to the right
  5. Each successful capture flashes a green box and adds a thumbnail below. The counter climbs to 3/3.
  6. Press Save student.
Why three samples from three angles? A single head-on template fails the moment a student tilts their head in a real lecture. Three angles give the matcher room to recognise the same person in a natural pose — this is the single biggest thing you can do to improve accuracy in the hall.
If capture keeps failing “No face detected” almost always means the light is behind the student. Turn them to face a window or a lamp. “Face is unclear” means they are too far away or moving.

4.5  Student profile

Everyone /students/[id]

Everything recorded about one student.

  • Left panel — photo, details, template status, and the courses they are registered on.
  • Attendance history — the last 50 records with course, session, time, status and whether it was face or manual.
  • Re-enrol face — captures three fresh samples and replaces the stored ones. Use this when a student is repeatedly not recognised, or their appearance has changed.
  • Delete — removes the student, their face template and all their attendance. Admin only, and it cannot be undone.

4.6  Courses

Everyone /courses

Administrators see every course; lecturers see only their own.

  1. Press + New course to open the creation form.
  2. Enter the course code and title (required), then level, credit unit, department and the lecturer who will teach it.
  3. Set Required attendance (%) — the threshold used to decide exam eligibility. The default is 75.
  4. Press Create course, then click the new card to add students to it.

4.7  Course detail & class list

Everyone /courses/[id]

The class list matters more than it looks: only students registered on a course are candidates for recognition in that course's lectures. This is what stops a student from another class walking past the camera and being marked present.

  1. Press + Add students to open the picker.
  2. Search, then tick each student you want. A red no face badge warns you that a student has no template and will not be recognised.
  3. Press Register N student(s), then Done.
  4. Use Remove beside any name to take them off the course.
  5. Start lecture session (top right) begins a sitting and jumps straight to the camera.

4.8  Lecture sessions

Everyone /sessions

A session is one sitting of one lecture. Attendance can only be recorded into an open session.

  1. Choose the course from the dropdown — it shows how many students are registered on each.
  2. Type the venue (for example LT1) so the register is identifiable later.
  3. Set Late after (mins). Anyone recognised after this many minutes is marked late instead of present. Default 15.
  4. Press Start session & open camera.

The table below lists every session. Open ones offer Capture and Close; closed ones offer View register.

One open session per course If a session is already open for that course, the system takes you to it rather than creating a duplicate — so a face can never be captured into the wrong sitting.

4.9  Live capture — taking attendance

Everyone /sessions/[id]/capture

The screen you will spend your lecture on.

  1. Wait for the button to read Start capture rather than Loading face detector…, then press it and allow camera access.
  2. A red SCANNING badge appears. The system now analyses a frame roughly every second.
  3. Point the camera at the students. Each detected face gets a coloured box and a name label.
  4. Watch the right-hand column: Present counts up, students move from Not yet marked to Marked present as they are recognised.
  5. For anyone the camera cannot pick up, press Mark manually beside their name.
  6. When the lecture ends press Close session to lock the register.

What the box colours mean

Green — newly marked Recognised and just saved to the register. The count on the right goes up.
Blue — already marked Recognised, but already recorded for this session. Nothing changes — safe to stay in frame.
Red — not recognised Either not registered on this course, or has no face template enrolled.
Getting the best results in a hall Faces need to be reasonably large in the frame, so one camera at the front will not reach the back row. The most reliable arrangement is to have students walk past the camera as they come in, or to pan the camera along each row in turn while you set up.
The frames counter “N frames scanned · N faces seen” under the video tells you the system is genuinely working even when nobody is in view. If frames are not climbing, capture is paused.

4.10  Session register

Everyone /sessions/[id]

The formal record of one lecture — who was present, who was late, who was absent.

  • Present table — each student with the time they were marked, their status, and the method. Face records also show the match distance in brackets: lower means a stronger match.
  • Absent panel — everyone on the class list who was never marked.
  • Export CSV — downloads the register, absentees included, for your records.
  • Remove — deletes a single record if it was captured in error.
  • Close / Reopen session — locks or unlocks the register.

4.11  Attendance report

Everyone /reports

The whole semester for one course on a single grid — this is the screen to show during a demonstration or defence.

  1. Pick a course from the dropdown.
  2. Read the four summary cards: sessions held, students registered, average attendance, and how many fall below the requirement.
  3. Read the grid — one row per student, one column per lecture:
    • P (green) — present  ·  L (amber) — late  ·  A (red) — absent
  4. The % column and the Eligibility badge show whether each student meets the course requirement.
  5. Press Export CSV for a spreadsheet copy of the entire grid.

4.12  Staff accounts

Admin only /staff

Where lecturers and administrators are created. This item does not appear in the sidebar for lecturers.

  1. Fill in full name, email and a password of at least six characters.
  2. Choose the role — Lecturer for teaching staff, Administrator for full access.
  3. Optionally add a staff ID and department.
  4. Press Create account. The new account appears in the list on the right and can sign in immediately.

7. Troubleshooting

The problems you are most likely to hit, and what to do about them.

SymptomWhy it happensWhat to do
The camera button stays greyed outThe face models (~12 MB) are still downloading.Wait 10–40 seconds. If it never finishes, reload the page — a partial download is retried from scratch.
“Camera permission was denied”The browser blocked access, usually from an earlier refusal.Click the padlock in the address bar, set Camera to Allow, then reload.
Nothing happens when you turn the camera onThe page is on an insecure address such as a LAN IP.Use the HTTPS live link, or localhost if running it yourself. See section 2.3.
“No face detected” during enrolmentBacklighting, or the student is too far away.Face the student towards the light, move to about an arm's length, and stay still while capturing.
A red box — a real student is not recognisedThey have no face template, or are not on this course's class list.Check the Students page for a red Not enrolled badge, and the course class list. Mark them manually for now, and re-enrol afterwards.
Recognised, but marked lateThey were recognised after the late cut-off for the session.Expected behaviour. Set a longer cut-off when starting the session if your lectures fill slowly.
“This session is closed”Attendance cannot be added to a finished lecture.Open the session register and press Reopen session if it was closed by mistake.
“This course already has an open session”A previous sitting was never closed.The system takes you to that session. Close it, then start a new one.
“Register students on this course first”The class list is empty.Open the course and add students before starting a session.
The report grid is emptyNo sessions have been held for that course yet.Run at least one lecture session. Only sessions that exist appear as columns.
Attendance stops being recorded mid-lectureCapture was paused, or the session was closed in another tab.Check for the red SCANNING badge and that the frames counter is climbing.

Known limits of the prototype

Worth stating plainly, especially during a demonstration:

  • Lighting still matters. In a dim hall the detector finds fewer faces. Manual marking exists for exactly this reason.
  • Range is limited. Faces must be reasonably large in frame; one camera at the front will not reach the back of a 300-seat hall.
  • There is no liveness detection. A printed photograph held to the camera would pass. Defeating that needs blink or depth sensing, which is outside this prototype's scope.
  • The threshold is a trade-off, not a certainty. Stricter settings reject more genuine students; looser settings risk matching the wrong one.
  • Matching scales linearly with class size — fine for tens or low hundreds of students per course.
